# 02 — Curriculum

Five pillars, two age bands, one map. This is the scope-and-sequence sketch — enough to
size the content build and to check that the pillars actually interlock.

## The interlock principle

Pillars are not silos. Every piece of content is tagged with **place**, **time**, **faith**
(where relevant) and **Hindi words**, so the same object shows up in four different journeys:

> **Sanchi Stupa** → lights up a dot in *Madhya Pradesh* on the map (Bhugol) · sits at
> ~250 BCE on the River of Time (Itihaas) · is where Ashoka's story lands (Itihaas) · carries
> Jataka tales carved on its gateways (Kathayein) · introduces *stupa*, *dhamma*, *Sangha*
> (Dharma) · and teaches the words **स्तूप**, **सम्राट** (Bhasha).

One well-built content object earns its cost five times. Build the tagging schema before the
content.

## Pillar 1 · Itihaas — Indian history

**Free core** (the story of each era) · **Premium depth** (mastery ladders, primary sources,
timeline challenges).

Rendered as the **River of Time (Kaal Nadi)** — a river the child sails down, each era a
bend, evidence washing up on the banks.

| Era | Roughly | Chhote (4–7) gets | Bade (8–12) gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harappan / Indus Valley | 3300–1300 BCE | The city with the great bath, the toy bullock cart, the dancing girl | Town planning, the undeciphered script, trade with Mesopotamia, why cities end |
| Vedic period | 1500–500 BCE | Cows, chants, fire, forest schools | Vedas as oral literature, gurukul, early society, the rivers of the hymns |
| Mahajanapadas & the Buddha's age | 600–300 BCE | The prince who left the palace | 16 kingdoms, republics, Magadha's rise, urbanisation, new ideas (Buddhism, Jainism) |
| Maurya | 322–185 BCE | The emperor who was sorry | Chandragupta & Chanakya, Ashoka's edicts and the pillars, the Lion Capital → our national emblem |
| Gupta & the classical age | 320–550 CE | Zero! Stars! Poems! | Aryabhata, decimal system, Kalidasa, Nalanda, Ajanta |
| The Souths | 300 BCE–1600 CE | Bronze dancing Shiva, sea-going ships | Cholas & the Southeast Asia connection, Pallava temples, Vijayanagara, Sangam poetry |
| Delhi Sultanate | 1206–1526 | Qutub Minar is *tall* | Sultans, Persian influence, architecture, the Bhakti–Sufi ferment |
| Mughal | 1526–1857 | Taj Mahal, gardens, miniature paintings | Akbar to Aurangzeb, administration, art, syncretism and conflict, decline |
| Marathas, Sikhs & regional powers | 1600–1850 | Forts! Horses! | Shivaji, Maratha confederacy, the Sikh Gurus & Ranjit Singh, Ahoms, Travancore |
| Colonial India | 1757–1947 | *(11–12 only)* | Company rule, 1857, economic drain, railways-and-what-they-cost, famines, reform movements |
| Freedom movement | 1885–1947 | Gandhi's stick and salt; Bhagat Singh's courage (light touch) | Congress, Gandhi, Ambedkar, Bose, Nehru, Patel, mass movements, Partition (11–12) |
| Modern India | 1947– | Flag, Republic Day, ISRO rockets | Constitution, Ambedkar's role, states reorganisation, Green Revolution, IT, space, today |

**Anchors used throughout:** 50 people, 50 places, 50 objects. Objects are the best hook for
this age — a seal, a coin, a pillar edict, a bronze, a sword, a spinning wheel, a rocket.

## Pillar 2 · Dharma — the faiths of India

**Free.** Presented from the inside — each tradition as its followers understand and practise
it — never ranked, never compared as "which is right." See
[05 — Editorial Policy](05-editorial-policy.md).

### Hinduism
Ideas at a child's level (dharma, karma, many paths, the divine in everything) · the deities
kids meet first (Ganesha, Krishna, Rama, Hanuman, Durga, Lakshmi, Saraswati, Shiva, Vishnu) ·
temples and how to be in one · puja at home · festivals through the year · texts (Vedas,
Upanishads, Gita, Puranas, Ramayana, Mahabharata) · pilgrimages · Bhakti saints (Mirabai,
Kabir, Tulsidas, the Alvars and Nayanars) · regional variety — because a Tamil, Bengali,
Gujarati and Punjabi Hindu childhood look genuinely different, and diaspora kids need to see
their *own* version represented.

### Buddhism
Siddhartha's story · the Four Noble Truths and Eightfold Path in kid-language · Sangha ·
stupas and viharas · Ashoka's spread of the dhamma · Jataka tales (a huge, ready-made story
library) · Buddhism today in Ladakh, Sikkim, Arunachal, Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia, Japan ·
Buddha Purnima · the Dalai Lama and Dharamshala · Ambedkar and the modern Navayana movement.

### Jainism
Ahimsa as a whole way of living (the idea a 6-year-old grasps fastest and remembers longest)
· Mahavira and the 24 Tirthankaras · anekantavada — the blind men and the elephant, which is
a *Jain* parable · Jain diet and why · Paryushan and Michhami Dukkadam · Shravanabelagola,
Palitana, Ranakpur, Dilwara · Jain manuscript painting · Jains today.

### Sikhism
Taught at the same depth as the three above, because a very large share of the diaspora
audience *is* Sikh and an app called Bizzing India that skips the Gurus will be noticed.

**Ik Onkar** — one God, the opening words of the Guru Granth Sahib and the idea everything
else hangs off · the **ten Gurus**, from Guru Nanak's travels to Guru Gobind Singh · the
**Guru Granth Sahib as the living Guru** — not a book *about* the faith but the Guru itself,
which is why it is carried, seated and attended the way it is · **langar and seva** — the
free kitchen where everyone sits in one row and eats the same food, and service as worship,
which is the fastest idea in this pillar for a child to grasp and act on · the **Khalsa** and
the **five Ks** (kesh, kangha, kara, kachhera, kirpan) — what each one means to the person
wearing it, which is also the answer to the playground question a Sikh child gets asked ·
**Harmandir Sahib**, the Golden Temple at Amritsar — four doors, the sarovar, its langar ·
**Baisakhi** and the **Gurpurabs** — the Guru anniversaries the household
year is built around · the **gurdwara** and how to be in one.

**Sikh diaspora history** gets real space, because for most of this audience it is family
history: Punjab → Canada, the UK, the US and Australia, and the gurdwara as the first
institution a new community builds. A Sikh child in Surrey, Southall or Yuba City should be
able to find their own grandparents' route on the map. Dates, routes and numbers here are
Itihaas, not Katha — sourced and badged like any other history ([05](05-editorial-policy.md)).

**The Gurmukhi connection.** Gurmukhi is the script of the Guru Granth Sahib, which makes
this pillar and the language platform reinforce each other directly — Punjabi is language
pack #2 ([09](09-language-engine.md#6-language-sequencing)), and it ships alongside this
pillar so faith and language land as one story.

### The wider weave *(survey level, and it matters)*
Islam, Christianity, Judaism and Zoroastrianism in India, each as an Indian story: Sufi
dargahs, Kerala's St Thomas churches and Cochin's synagogue, Parsis and Mumbai. India is a
place where these have lived side by side for centuries, and a child in Edison or Slough
sits next to classmates from all of them.

## Pillar 3 · Kathayein — mythology & story

**Free. The front door of the entire product.** Highest emotional return, lowest resistance,
best shareability, straight line to the book business.

Hub: the **Story Tree (Katha Vriksha)**.

| Collection | Volume | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ramayana** | ~24 episodes | Serialised, from Rama's birth to the return to Ayodhya. Note regional Ramayanas (Valmiki, Tulsidas, Kamban, Southeast Asian versions) for 10+ |
| **Mahabharata** | ~30 episodes | Serialised. The Gita handled at 10+ as "a talk about doing the right thing when it's hard" |
| **Puranic tales** | ~40 | Ganesha's tusk, Krishna and the butter, Shiva and Ganga, Durga and Mahishasura, the churning of the ocean, Narasimha, Vamana, Markandeya |
| **Jataka tales** | ~40 | The Buddha's previous lives. Short, moral, animal-led — perfect for Chhote mode |
| **Jain stories** | ~20 | Mahavira's life, Bahubali and Bharata, the elephant parable, Chandanbala |
| **Panchatantra & Hitopadesha** | ~40 | The world's most-travelled story collection — worth telling kids *that* |
| **Folk & regional** | ~40 | Vikram–Betaal, Tenali Rama, Birbal, Baital Pachisi, Bengali *thakurmar jhuli*, Rajasthani Phad ballads, Northeast and Adivasi tales |
| **Epics of the South & the seas** | ~15 | Silappadikaram, Chola voyages, Ramayana in Java and Thailand |

Every story ships with: narration (EN + HI), 4–8 folk-art illustrations, a 3-question
comprehension beat, a **Katha** badge, place/time tags, and a "read it in print" card.

**Chhote mode:** 3–5 minutes, listen-and-tap, no reading needed.
**Bade mode:** read-along with text, plus the "where does this story live?" map link and
"which version did *your* family hear?" prompt.

## Pillar 4 · Bhugol — geography

**Free core** (the map, states, rivers) · **Premium depth** (mastery ladders, printables).

**The Living Map is the curriculum.** Everything below is a layer you can toggle on it.

- **States & UTs** — 28 + 8, capitals, shapes, one signature thing each (food, dance, animal,
  festival, monument, language). This is the single most-repeated quiz surface in the app.
- **Rivers** — Ganga, Yamuna, Brahmaputra, Indus, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Narmada,
  Mahanadi, Tapti. Where they rise, where they meet the sea, the cities on them, why
  civilisations sit on them.
- **Mountains & landforms** — Himalaya, Western & Eastern Ghats, Aravalli (older than the
  Himalaya, which delights kids), Vindhya, Thar, Deccan plateau, Sundarbans, Rann of Kutch.
- **Monsoon** — the one system that explains Indian agriculture, festivals, food and history.
  Worth a whole unit: where the wind comes from, why June, why it matters.
- **Wildlife** — tiger, elephant, one-horned rhino, lion of Gir, snow leopard, Nilgiri tahr,
  Olive Ridley turtles, peacock, hornbill. Habitat maps and conservation.
- **Monuments** — 40+ pinned: Taj, Qutub, Hampi, Khajuraho, Konark, Meenakshi, Golden Temple,
  Sanchi, Ajanta & Ellora, Ranakpur, Amber, Charminar, Victoria Terminus, Sun Temple, Rani ki Vav.
- **Food map** — the fastest way to a 6-year-old's attention. Dosa, dhokla, litti chokha,
  rogan josh, macher jhol, puran poli, bisi bele bath, momos.
- **Festival map** — which festival, which region, which month, why.
- **Language map** — 22 scheduled languages, the four families, which script goes with which.
  Explicitly sets up the Hindi pillar *and* respects that a Tamil or Bengali family's home
  language isn't Hindi. Handle this well; get it wrong and you lose half the diaspora.

## Pillar 5 · Bhasha — language *(Premium)*

The paid pillar. Must be a **real language program**, not vocabulary flashcards, because
it's competing with a human tutor. It is also the pillar with the most careful audio
requirements.

**Bhasha is a platform, and [09 — The Bhasha Engine](09-language-engine.md) is the document
for it** — script modules vs language packs, the heritage learner, typing-free exercise
design, language sequencing, per-pack cost and pricing. The stage ladder below is the shared
curriculum shape; **Hindi/Devanagari is pack #1**, Punjabi/Gurmukhi is #2, and every pack runs
the same stages against its own script and lexicon. Don't duplicate 09 here — extend it there.

**Design commitments**
- **Script from day one** (not romanised-forever) — Devanagari for Hindi, Gurmukhi for
  Punjabi, and so on. Script literacy is the outcome parents actually want and can see.
- **Two entry paths**: heritage learner (understands some spoken Hindi, can't read) and
  true beginner. The heritage path is the diaspora default and almost nobody builds it.
- **Speaking is the goal**, so speech practice and family voice matter more than typing.
- **Human voice** for every letter, matra and core word. Nothing a child will imitate gets
  synthesised.

The ladder, with pack #1 (Hindi) as the worked example:

| Stage | Content | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 0 · Sunna | 200 spoken words & 30 phrases, listen-and-point, no script | Ear first. Chhote mode lives here |
| 1 · Varnamala | 13 svar + 33 vyanjan, stroke-order writing, sound families | Recognises and writes every letter |
| 2 · Matras | All 12 matras, barakhadi grids, joining letters | Reads any simple word aloud |
| 3 · Shabd | 1,000 core words by theme — family, food, body, home, school, animals, colours, numbers, time | Reads and understands common words |
| 4 · Vakya | Sentence structure (SOV), gender, postpositions, verb agreement, tenses | Builds correct simple sentences |
| 5 · Baat-cheet | 40 conversation scenarios: greeting elders, the market, the phone call to Nani, at a wedding, ordering food | Holds a real short conversation |
| 6 · Padhna | Graded readers — Panchatantra, then folk tales, then a short Premchand adaptation | Reads a page of real Hindi |
| 7 · Likhna | Handwriting worksheets, dictation, short paragraph writing | Writes a paragraph |

**Bhasha Coach** is the trainer shell (the analogue of Bizzing Bee's Word Coach): a chosen
list, Revise and Practice tabs, a live heatmap, a Level ladder, spaced repetition on the
child's own misses, and printable worksheets — the last being a direct bridge to the
workbook business.

**Architecture note:** the language layer is **language-agnostic from day one** — the single
highest-leverage technical decision in the product. The diaspora is Tamil, Telugu, Gujarati,
Punjabi, Bengali and Marathi too, and a second language must be a content pack against the
same engine, not a rewrite. The specs, the sequencing and the gate that proves it:
[09 — The Bhasha Engine](09-language-engine.md).
